# Mobility Patterns — catalog for a daily routine

Companion to `Exercise Catalog.md` (strength). Mobility routines online look different but are made of ~15 patterns. Each "the only 5 stretches you need" video is a different sample from the same table. **Pick one move per pattern the person needs, fix the order, and stop collecting routines.**

The person's own routine lives in their `Practices`. This file is the pick-list plus the rules.

## Selection rules

1. **Morning: avoid end-range or repeated lumbar flexion for the first ~60 min.** Discs are fully hydrated after lying down, so bending forward hurts them most at this time (McGill; Adams & Dolan). Rows flagged `flex` belong later in the day. Extension, rotation with a neutral lower back, and hip work are fine.
2. **Active vs passive — choose by tissue, not by habit.**
   - **Passive (P)** — hold a position, let gravity or the hands do the work. It helps stiff people. For hypermobile people it adds little and stresses tissue that is already loose.
   - **Active (A)** — move into the range with your own muscles, 5–6 reps, 1–2 s pause at the end. Default for hypermobility and for the morning.
3. **Fixed beats varied.** Do the same sequence every day. It becomes automatic and costs no decision. It also works as a daily sensor: on a fixed routine you notice "the right side is stiffer today". On a rotating one you notice nothing. Change a move only when its pattern stops being needed or the move irritates something.
4. **Prefer compound moves.** One move can cover 2–3 patterns. **6–8 moves cover the table in 5–8 min.**
5. **Order: floor → standing** (prone/supine → side → seated/kneeling → squat → standing), so you never get up and down twice.
6. **Three different jobs — don't mix them:**
   - **Morning routine:** wake up and check the body. 5–8 min, mostly A.
   - **Pre-gym warm-up:** specific to that session.
   - **Evening stretch:** P allowed, relaxation.
   A 15–20 min follow-along "full-body stretch" is the evening job.

- **Mode:** A = active, P = passive.

- `flex` — lumbar flexion at end range.
- `knee` — deep knee flexion or frontal-plane load on the knee.
- `neck` — end-range cervical motion.
- `wrist` — weight on extended wrists.

## Building a routine (recipe)

1. From the person's profile, list the **patterns they need**: stiff areas, sitting load, sport, rehab direction.
2. Strike moves whose **flags** clash with their constraints. Strike `flex` moves if the routine is in the morning.
3. Pick **one move per needed pattern**, bold ones first, until each pattern is covered. Usually that's 6–8 moves.
4. **Group by position, not by move:** prone → supine → side → kneeling → squat → standing, which is the act of getting up off the floor. Remember 5–6 positions with 1–2 moves each, not a list of 8 exercises. Set reps: A moves 5–6 per side, P moves 2 × 15–30 s.
5. Write it into `Practices` as a fixed table, with one line on why each move is there for this person.

## Reading a routine you found

Map every move to a row above.

- **Duplicates:** if three moves land in the same pattern, keep one.
- **Gaps:** an empty pattern you need is the gap.
- **Wrong time of day:** a `flex` move is for later in the day, not the morning.

Example: three popular 5-move "morning routines" (15 moves) hit only ~9 patterns. QL appears in all three, and the deep-squat thoracic reach in two.
